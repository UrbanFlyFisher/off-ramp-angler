License & Limits

Anyone 16 or older needs a California sport fishing license to fish public waters, available online at CDFW's licensing site or at most sporting goods stores. Kids 15 and under fish free, always. Two "Free Fishing Days" each year waive the license requirement for everyone.

Public vs. Private Water

Most lakes on this list are city or county park lakes stocked by CDFW's Fishing in the City program — free to fish with a license. A few big draws like Irvine Lake and the Santa Ana River Lakes are privately operated and charge a day-use or rod fee, with their own posted rules and hours.

Core Tackle Kit

  • 4–8 lb line for trout/panfish, 10–15 lb for catfish and bass
  • Small split shots, size 8–12 hooks for bait, size 1–4 for catfish
  • Bobbers/floats, a few small swivels
  • PowerBait or nightcrawlers (trout), chicken liver or mackerel (catfish), plastic worms (bass)

Family-Day Basics

  • Shade, sunscreen, and refillable water — most of these lakes have little natural shade on open banks
  • Arrive at opening or an hour before dusk; midday summer heat is slow for everything but catfish
  • Bathrooms and paved paths at nearly every lake make these strong picks for strollers and younger kids
  • Always check current closures and stocking schedules — conditions change with drought and maintenance

Handling & Ethics

  • Wet your hands before handling fish you plan to release
  • Pack out all line, hooks, and bait containers — wildlife gets tangled in loose line
  • Respect posted bag limits and any lake-specific gear restrictions (some ban treble hooks or live minnows)

Stocking Windows

Across almost every lake here, trout go in from roughly late October/November through March when the water is cool, and catfish go in from spring through early fall when it's warm. Fishing is nearly always best in the day or two right after a truck stocks a lake — check CDFW's fish planting schedule before you head out.
